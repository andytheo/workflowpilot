How to organize client inquiries with a free form and spreadsheet

Build a simple client inquiry tracker with a form, spreadsheet, and follow-up status.

This guide describes a workflow you can reproduce. It is not a claim of a completed product test.

If customer requests arrive through scattered messages, a shared intake form and spreadsheet can make follow-up more consistent without buying a CRM.

1. Define the fields

Create fields for name, contact method, what the customer needs, and a short description. Avoid collecting sensitive information you don't need.

2. Connect a form to a spreadsheet

Use a form builder that supports saving responses to a spreadsheet. Create a test submission and verify the fields appear in the correct columns.

3. Add a follow-up process

Add columns named Status, Assigned to, Next follow-up and Notes. Choose a small set of statuses such as New, In progress and Closed.

4. Test before sharing

Submit several fake inquiries, check mobile usability, test access permissions and ensure only authorized people can see the responses.

When to upgrade

A spreadsheet is not ideal when you need audit trails, permissions by role, sophisticated automations or high volumes. At that point compare CRMs based on your actual requirements.

Quick checklist

  • Use a clear privacy notice.
  • Collect only necessary information.
  • Restrict spreadsheet access.
  • Set a regular follow-up routine.
